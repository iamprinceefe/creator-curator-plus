# Creator Curator Plus

MASTER PROMPT: BUILD A PREMIUM CREATOR / CONTENT CREATOR PORTFOLIO FROM AN INSTAGRAM PROFILE

Instagram Profile: https://www.instagram.com/ellaroli_?xtok=d2hoeWR6MWZxajJw

1. YOUR ROLE



Act as a senior creative director, personal brand strategist, award-winning portfolio designer, UX/UI designer, conversion-focused web designer, and frontend engineer.



Your task is to analyze the Instagram profile I provide and transform the creator’s existing online presence, content, visual identity, and personal brand into a premium, world-class Creator / Content Creator Portfolio.



Do not create a generic influencer landing page or a basic portfolio template. I want something that feels like it was designed by a high-end digital creative agency for a creator who works with established brands, premium businesses, marketing agencies, and ambitious startups.



The final product must be visually sophisticated, strategically positioned, technically polished, and designed to convert visitors into collaboration inquiries.



Think of the portfolio as a combination of:



* A premium personal brand website.

* A professional creator media kit.

* A visual content showcase.

* A UGC and brand-collaboration portfolio.

* A conversion-focused business development tool.



Your objective is to make the creator look credible, distinctive, commercially valuable, and ready for professional brand partnerships.

Instagram profile URL: [INSERT INSTAGRAM PROFILE URL HERE]



Creator’s name: Extract from the profile where possible.

Primary market: Determine from available evidence. Do not assume the creator’s location or audience demographics without supporting information.



Primary objective: Build a premium creator portfolio that attracts brand partnerships, paid content opportunities, UGC projects, campaign collaborations, and professional inquiries.

3. ACCESS, RESEARCH, AND DATA EXTRACTION



First, attempt to access the Instagram profile using the tools, browser, integrations, or research capabilities available to you.



Review the following where publicly accessible:



1. Profile name and username.

2. Profile biography and positioning.

3. Profile picture and branding.

4. Bio links and other publicly linked platforms.

5. Content categories and recurring themes.

6. Reels, videos, and other visual content.

7. Captions and storytelling style.

8. Visual consistency, editing style, and overall aesthetic.

9. Brands, products, services, and locations featured in the content.

10. Public engagement indicators, including visible likes, views, comments, and follower count.

11. Content formats the creator appears strongest in.

12. Potential commercial applications of the creator’s content.

13. The creator’s personality, communication style, and distinctive strengths.

14. Existing portfolio, website, or collaboration information linked from the profile.



Look beyond the number of followers. Assess the creator’s potential value to a brand based on content quality, presentation, storytelling, niche relevance, production style, authenticity, and potential campaign applications.



Important access rules



Instagram may restrict access to profiles, Reels, captions, or engagement information.



If direct access is unavailable:



* Do not claim to have accessed content you could not see.

* Do not invent the creator’s biography, achievements, metrics, brand partnerships, audience demographics, or performance results.

* Use any genuinely accessible public information.

* Tell me precisely what additional information or assets you need from me.

* Continue building the portfolio using verified information and clearly marked placeholders where necessary.



If I provide screenshots, downloaded videos, images, captions, analytics screenshots, or other profile materials, use those materials as primary references for the portfolio.



Do not attempt to bypass Instagram’s login requirements, access controls, or privacy restrictions.

4. DEVELOP A STRONG CREATOR BRAND STRATEGY



Before writing the website content, determine the creator’s most compelling professional positioning.



Answer these questions internally and use the findings to guide the design:



* Who is this creator?

* What niche or combination of niches do they occupy?

* What makes their content distinctive?

* What type of brands would benefit most from working with them?

* What content formats can they offer?

* What problems can their content solve for brands?

* What visual identity best represents their existing content?

* What would make a marketing manager trust this creator?

* What would motivate a brand to request their media kit or book a collaboration?

* What makes this creator different from thousands of other creators?



Develop a concise brand positioning statement.



Create a compelling hero headline and supporting statement that communicate the creator’s value within seconds.



Avoid generic phrases such as “I am passionate about creating content” unless they are supported by a meaningful, distinctive message.



The copy should be confident, sophisticated, personable, and commercially aware without sounding boastful or artificial.



Do not change the creator’s personality into something inconsistent with the actual Instagram profile.



⸻



5. DESIGN DIRECTION: PREMIUM, MODERN, AND EDITORIAL



The website must look like a professionally art-directed digital experience rather than a conventional portfolio template.



Use a design language informed by premium creative studios, contemporary editorial websites, fashion campaigns, luxury brand storytelling, and high-quality digital portfolios.



Visual direction



Prioritize:



* Sophisticated typography.

* Strong visual hierarchy.

* Beautifully composed layouts.

* Intentional whitespace.

* Large, high-quality creator imagery.

* Editorial-style image grids.

* Balanced proportions and refined spacing.

* Subtle, polished motion.

* Thoughtful transitions.

* Consistent visual rhythm.

* Strong mobile responsiveness.

* A restrained, cohesive colour palette.



Study the creator’s existing Instagram aesthetic before choosing the final design direction.



If the creator’s identity is vibrant and expressive, reflect that in a controlled, sophisticated way. If it is minimal, editorial, luxurious, or understated, preserve that character.



Do not force every creator into a black-and-white luxury aesthetic.



Typography



Use a refined typography system with:



* A distinctive display typeface for major headlines.

* A highly readable typeface for body copy.

* Carefully controlled font sizes and line lengths.

* Clear differentiation between headings, supporting text, metadata, and buttons.



Use high-quality, web-accessible fonts where possible. Avoid excessive font combinations.



Colour system



Create a cohesive palette based on the creator’s brand and content.



Define:



* Primary background colour.

* Secondary background colour.

* Main text colour.

* Muted text colour.

* Accent colour.

* Interactive states.



Maintain sufficient contrast and accessibility.



Layout and composition



Use asymmetrical editorial compositions where appropriate, intentional image crops, overlapping elements only when visually justified, and a grid system that makes the work feel curated.



Avoid excessive rounded cards, generic gradients, unnecessary glassmorphism, decorative blobs, random icons, and overused template-style sections.



Every design decision should have a purpose.

6. WEBSITE STRUCTURE AND REQUIRED SECTIONS



Build a complete, polished portfolio with the following sections, adapting them to the creator’s actual niche and available information.



A. Navigation



Create an elegant, minimal navigation bar.



Include:



* Creator’s name or wordmark.

* Work.

* About.

* Services or Collaborations.

* Contact.

* A prominent collaboration inquiry button.



Navigation should be intuitive, responsive, and functional.



On mobile, create a clean mobile navigation menu.



B. Hero section



The first screen must immediately communicate the creator’s identity and value.



Include:



* Creator’s name.

* A compelling professional headline.

* A concise description of their creative niche.

* A strong hero image, video, or visual composition sourced from the creator’s available content.

* A primary call to action: “Work With Me” or a more appropriate alternative.

* A secondary action: “Explore My Work.”



If supported by the available evidence, add a small contextual line describing the creator’s specialty or location.



The hero should feel memorable and intentional. Do not overcrowd it with statistics, badges, and unnecessary text.



C. Featured work / content portfolio



This is one of the most important sections.



Use the actual Instagram content wherever possible.



Select the strongest and most relevant examples rather than simply displaying everything.



Create a curated visual gallery that may include:



* Featured Reels.

* Lifestyle photography.

* Product demonstrations.

* Fashion or beauty content.

* Brand-focused storytelling.

* UGC-style videos.

* Tutorials.

* Campaign-style visuals.

* Other formats that fit the creator’s niche.



Each featured item should have an attractive visual preview and, where supported, information such as:



* Content title.

* Content category.

* Brand or product featured.

* Content format.

* A concise description of the creative approach.

* A link to the original public Instagram post, if available.



Clicking an item should open an appropriate detail view, video player, lightbox, or original post.



Do not invent campaign names or imply that an organic post was a paid brand collaboration.



Where appropriate, label work honestly as “Featured Content,” “Product Feature,” “Organic Content,” “UGC Sample,” or “Brand Campaign.”



Distinguish clearly between actual commissioned work and independently created content.



D. About the creator



Create a concise, well-written introduction based on the creator’s real profile and available materials.



The section should communicate:



* The creator’s identity.

* Their creative niche.

* Their approach to content creation.

* Their interests or perspective, where relevant.

* The type of stories, products, or experiences they enjoy bringing to life.

* What brands can expect when collaborating with them.



Use a natural first-person voice where appropriate.



Avoid generic biographies and unsupported claims about experience, awards, clients, or industry recognition.



Use an authentic creator photograph or another appropriate visual from the available assets.



E. Content capabilities and services



Identify relevant services based on the creator’s niche and content style.



Potential offerings include:



* User-generated content (UGC).

* Short-form video production.

* Instagram Reels.

* Product demonstrations.

* Product reviews and unboxings.

* Lifestyle photography.

* Fashion or beauty content.

* Brand storytelling.

* Tutorials and educational content.

* Social media campaign assets.

* Creative concepts and content direction.

* Event coverage.

* Sponsored social content.

* Testimonial-style videos.



These are examples, not a checklist that must all be included.



Only present services that are supported by the creator’s actual skills or can reasonably be offered with their approval.



Separate UGC production from influencer publishing. A creator may produce content for a brand without posting it to their own account.



Explain the potential value of each selected service in concise, brand-oriented language.



Do not publish pricing unless I provide approved rates or explicitly request a pricing structure.



F. Niche and brand fit



Identify the industries that genuinely fit the creator’s content.



Examples may include fashion, beauty, skincare, lifestyle, food, travel, hospitality, consumer technology, fitness, or other relevant categories.



Display suitable categories through a refined visual treatment.



Do not claim that the creator has worked with brands in a category unless there is evidence. You may describe a category as a potential collaboration area instead.



Make it easy for a prospective brand to see how the creator could fit into its marketing strategy.



G. Performance and creator statistics



If verified information is available, display relevant statistics such as:



* Instagram follower count.

* Average Reel views.

* Engagement rate.

* Audience location.

* Audience age distribution.

* Audience gender distribution.

* Average reach.

* Monthly impressions.

* Relevant campaign performance.



Use only verified data.



Clearly indicate when statistics were last updated if the date is known.



Do not fabricate figures or calculate an engagement rate from incomplete information.



If analytics are unavailable, omit this section or create a visually polished, optional metrics section that remains unpublished until verified data is supplied.



Never substitute invented metrics for missing data.



H. Brand collaborations and testimonials



If genuine partnerships, client names, campaign examples, or testimonials are available, present them elegantly.



Use a restrained logo strip, project highlights, short testimonial excerpts, or campaign case studies where appropriate.



Only use brand logos and testimonials when they are legitimate and appropriate to display.



Never invent testimonials, client endorsements, campaign results, or past partnerships.



If the creator has no documented commercial collaborations, focus on the quality of their featured work and their potential to create effective brand content.



I. Creative process



Create a concise section explaining how working with the creator could proceed.



For example:



1. Discovery — understand the brand, goals, audience, and brief.

2. Concept — develop creative direction and content ideas.

3. Production — create the agreed content.

4. Delivery — supply the final assets and any agreed revisions.



Adapt the steps to the services actually offered.



Keep the section simple, reassuring, and professional.



J. Collaboration inquiry / contact section



Design a compelling closing section that encourages brands to make contact.



Include:



* A strong, inviting headline.

* A concise collaboration message.

* A clear email contact method, if provided.

* Instagram and other verified social links.

* A functional inquiry form, if the technical setup supports it.

* A clear call to action.



If a form is implemented, include fields such as:



* Name.

* Work email.

* Brand or company.

* Collaboration type.

* Project details.

* Optional budget range.



Implement appropriate validation, clear success and error states, and a real submission destination.



Do not create a form that merely looks functional but silently discards submissions.



If no backend or form service is configured, provide a working email link or a clearly documented integration point instead.



K. Footer



Include:



* Creator’s name or wordmark.

* Relevant navigation links.

* Verified social links.

* Contact details where approved.

* Copyright notice with the current year.

* A restrained design consistent with the rest of the site.



Avoid clutter.

7. USE REAL INSTAGRAM CONTENT, NOT PLACEHOLDERS



Treat the creator’s content as the visual foundation of the entire website.



Prioritize genuine images, videos, thumbnails, and other media supplied by the creator or accessible through legitimate public sources.



For each selected asset:



* Preserve the creator’s identity.

* Maintain the correct aspect ratio.

* Use appropriate responsive image sizing.

* Apply intentional crops without distorting the subject.

* Avoid excessive compression and visibly poor-quality previews.

* Provide suitable alternative text for meaningful images.

* Use appropriate loading strategies for performance.



For video content, use suitable thumbnails, play controls, and accessible video playback where technically possible.



If Instagram’s embedding or media-access restrictions prevent direct use, do not pretend the media has been downloaded or embedded successfully.



Instead, use creator-supplied media, approved public embed options, or links to the original posts.



Never hotlink private assets or use unauthorized methods to extract protected content.



Do not use unrelated stock photos to represent the creator.



If a suitable asset is unavailable, design a deliberate placeholder that can easily be replaced.



⸻



8. ADVANCED INTERACTION AND MOTION DESIGN



Add sophisticated interactions that improve the experience without distracting from the work.



Consider:



* Smooth anchor navigation.

* Refined hover effects on portfolio items.

* Subtle image transitions.

* Scroll-triggered reveals used sparingly.

* Elegant page-load transitions where appropriate.

* A polished mobile menu.

* Interactive project previews.

* Lightboxes or dedicated project views.

* Subtle button feedback.

* Video playback controls.

* Thoughtful transitions between content states.



Motion should feel smooth, restrained, and intentional.



Respect reduced-motion accessibility preferences.



Avoid excessive animation, distracting cursor effects, unnecessary 3D objects, constant movement, and animations that slow down the website.



Do not sacrifice performance for visual effects.



⸻



9. TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION



If you have access to a coding environment, build the actual website rather than merely describing a proposed design.



First inspect the existing project files, framework, dependencies, and available assets. Reuse a suitable existing setup rather than replacing working infrastructure unnecessarily.



If no project exists, select an appropriate modern stack that you can implement and maintain reliably.



For example:



* React with Next.js or another suitable React framework.

* TypeScript where supported.

* Tailwind CSS or a coherent custom styling system.

* Reusable, well-structured components.

* A suitable image optimization strategy.

* Accessible semantic HTML.

* A lightweight animation library only if genuinely useful.



Do not install unnecessary dependencies or introduce complexity without a clear benefit.



Build a complete, runnable website with real page structure, styling, content, and working interactions.



The website should include:



* Responsive desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts.

* Clean component architecture.

* Consistent design tokens.

* Reusable portfolio components.

* Appropriate image loading and sizing.

* Accessible keyboard navigation.

* Visible focus states.

* Semantic headings.

* Form validation where relevant.

* Sensible error handling.

* Search engine metadata.

* Social sharing metadata.

* A favicon or creator-specific site icon where appropriate.

* A custom 404 page if supported by the framework.

* A privacy-conscious analytics integration point if needed.



Do not expose API keys, private tokens, credentials, or other secrets in client-side code.



Use environment variables for sensitive configuration and document any required setup.



If the website requires external services, identify those dependencies and explain how to configure them.
